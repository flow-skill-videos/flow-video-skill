# Video 002 — Pep Tonic Outdoor Bizarre Hook

## Status

Validated end-to-end workflow.

This example records the structural lessons from a second UGC production. It is not a fixed script that must be reused word-for-word.

## Goal

Increase retention by moving from a relatively ordinary visual hook to a bizarre, contradictory, curiosity-first visual that still has a logical bridge into the product story.

## Core creative mechanism

The first frame uses:
- mature recurring male character
- shirtless presentation because it fit this specific body/wellness visual concept
- bright outdoor daylight
- close, slightly wide-angle creator-camera perspective
- oversized bizarre swollen-belly metaphor prop dominating the foreground
- no product in the hook
- direct eye contact and slight lean toward camera

The prop is intentionally strange enough to create a "what is that?" reaction, but it is not random. Scene 2 explains the metaphor, Scene 3 transitions away from the hook, and Scene 4 reveals the product.

## Scene structure

### Scene 1 — 6 seconds — Bizarre hook
- oversized strange belly prop dominates lower foreground
- bright outdoor location
- close wide-angle perspective
- character leans toward camera
- subtle handheld movement and tiny push-in
- product hidden

### Scene 2 — 8 seconds — Explain/reframe
- same prop remains visible
- character framed slightly closer
- gestures between own abdomen and metaphor prop
- subtle handheld motion
- composition differs from Scene 1
- product still hidden

### Scene 3 — 8 seconds — Transition
- prop moved to one side and becomes secondary
- tighter face/upper-body framing
- dismissive gesture away from random fixes/complexity
- slight lateral camera shift and push-in
- product still hidden

### Scene 4 — 6 seconds — Product reveal/demo
- one master product container
- one scoop
- one glass
- camera subtly tilts/follows the scoop and pour
- product enters as the payoff to the previous transition
- strange hook prop stays only as a background continuity element

### Scene 5 — 8 seconds — Credibility/simple value
- finished drink in hand
- product visible but secondary
- much closer creator framing
- slight sideways drift
- subtle push-in near the key line
- no drinking action needed

### Scene 6 — 6 seconds — CTA
- closest framing of the sequence
- product naturally held at lower chest level
- finished drink remains visible
- subtle final push-in
- low-pressure creator recommendation rather than infomercial delivery

## Validated camera lessons

- Do not repeat the same locked medium shot for every scene.
- Change distance and composition between narrative blocks.
- Camera movement should follow the physical action or emotional beat.
- Use subtle handheld motion, not cinematic movement.
- Slightly wide-angle close framing makes foreground props feel larger.
- Product-demo scenes benefit from a small action-following tilt/reposition.
- Credibility and CTA scenes can become progressively closer.

## Validated hook lesson

An earlier idea using a large spread of fruits/vegetables was rejected because it was relevant but not bizarre enough for the reference style.

New rule:

> For reference styles built around aggressive curiosity, do not choose a hook merely because it relates to the product. Choose a bizarre or contradictory visual that creates immediate confusion/curiosity AND can be logically explained in the next scene.

The hook must satisfy:
1. pattern interrupt
2. semantic bridge
3. narrative payoff

## Validated generation bug fix

A video generation interpreted "smartphone UGC" as content displayed inside a phone frame.

Fix: every vulnerable video prompt should explicitly request full-frame exported footage and prohibit:
- phone frame
- bezel
- notch
- status icons
- app UI
- screen-within-a-screen
- device mockup
- black borders

Do not rely on the phrase "smartphone-style" by itself.

## Product consistency lesson

During action-heavy product scenes, explicitly specify:
- exactly one product container
- exactly one scoop
- exactly one glass

Use the master product reference throughout reveal, demonstration, credibility and CTA scenes.

## Lighting lesson

Bright outdoor natural daylight improved the perceived energy of this reference style compared with the more static indoor baseline.

Treat this as a style-specific option, not a universal requirement.

## Safety / claim note

The bizarre belly object is a visual metaphor, not medical proof. For health/wellness products, keep claims neutral, avoid invented diagnoses or guaranteed outcomes, and use verified product features/claims where needed.

## Production sequence used

Character/voice already existed → product master reference → script/scene plan → Scene 1 image → Scene 1 video → Scene 2 image → Scene 2 video → repeat through Scene 6 → hard-cut assembly → captions in post.
